How it works

pipeline · rebalance · heartbeat · lights

The pipeline

  1. Binance: two websocket connections. The order book for every coin (every 100 ms) on /public; trades, mark price and funding on /market. Latency is measured on every message.
  2. Collector (always on): closed candles 1m–1d, 1-minute buy/sell flow, the book every second, open interest every minute, funding / premium / long-short every 5 minutes. Gaps are re-fetched after any disconnect.
  3. Database: PostgreSQL with history since 2020, everything live, and the paper ledger.
  4. Strategy: the paper engine (see rebalance).
  5. Risk: caps of 3× gross and 0.5× per coin. A −3% day turns the engine reduce-only; a −30% drawdown flattens everything and halts until a manual restart.
  6. Execution (paper): a maker limit order, then taker. Simulated against the real order book; no real orders.
  7. Ledger: every decision, order, fill, fee and funding payment, plus an equity mark every minute. The account is rebuilt from these on restart.

How a rebalance works

  1. Every hour at HH:01:30 UTC the engine wakes up and checks the schedule. Only every 72nd hour (counted from 1970) is a rebalance; otherwise it logs HOLD.
  2. Safety first. Halted → NO_TRADE. More than half the order books older than 10 s → NO_TRADE. Last candle missing → NO_TRADE.
  3. Targets: the ridge forecast (retrained monthly), momentum rules and funding carry, mixed 50/25/25 and scaled to a 20% yearly swing, max 3× gross and 0.5× per coin.
  4. Orders: coins whose target moved less than 1% of equity are skipped. Sizes are rounded to Binance lot steps and minimum order values.
  5. Execution: a maker limit at the best price for 20 minutes, filled only by real trades through that price. The remainder goes taker after the measured latency.
  6. Costs: fees of 0.02% maker / 0.05% taker on each fill. Funding is paid or received at each settlement while a position is held.

What the heartbeat is

The engine writes a timestamp into the database once a minute (at second :01, after valuing the account) and after every hourly check. The dashboard shows its age.

  • under 2.5 min: the engine is alive
  • 2.5–10 min: late (busy or briefly stuck)
  • over 10 min: stopped or crashed (check journalctl -u alpha-paper)

It pauses on its own if no mark prices arrive from Binance, so a stale heartbeat can also mean a dead price feed.

Reading the lights

  • OK: normal
  • CHECK: slower or weaker than normal
  • PROBLEM: broken or stopped
  • WAITING: nothing expected yet
